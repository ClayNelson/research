## 1. Reinforced Theses

### **1.1 The “Continuous Arm Architecture” Thesis**
Your existing position that Arm’s strength comes from **continuity across dev → CI → infra** (M-series → Arm runners → Cobalt/Graviton) is reinforced.

New evidence:
- GitHub Arm64 runners (Linux/Windows) + Azure Cobalt GA tighten this chain.
- Python Software Foundation migration provides a mainstream validation case.
- Key ecosystem players frame Arm as the “optimized default” for *new* services.

**Revision:**  
Arm remains a **preferential default**, not a universal replacement.  
Multi-arch remains sticky in GPU-heavy clusters.

---

### **1.2 Edge AI as a “Vertical Stack” business**
Your world model already acknowledges verticalization in regulated workloads.

- Wind River, QNX, NVIDIA/Siemens are all releasing **stacked** offerings integrating RTOS + toolchain + accelerators + safety artifacts.
- EU and academic research emphasize hybrid HW/SW co-design but show gaps that only vertically-integrated vendors are solving today.

**Revision:**  
Edge AI is *even more* vertically-bound than previously assumed.

---

### **1.3 Federated agent control (multi-HQ world)**
You already have this theme implicitly.

New evidence:

- MCP is now “one protocol among several,” not the single one.

**Revision:**  
This federation is now a **structural inevitability**, not just a strategic hypothesis.

## 2. Challenged or Modified Theses

### **2.1 Regulated DevEx needs more than workflow orchestration**
Your current world model notes the importance of regulated DevEx, but the scan now suggests:

- DevEx in automotive/industrial = **evidence + certification + lifecycle**, not just better pipelines.
- Regulatory compliance is becoming a *core surface*, not an outer constraint.

**Revision:**  
Shift framing from “DevEx for regulated environments” →  
**“Evidence-generating development systems.”**

This language is more accurate and differentiating.

---

### **2.2 AI-native orgs won’t flatten—platform constraints increase first**
Your world model embraces platformization but assumes some complexity reduction.

New evidence:
- Most orgs are adding governance and AI platforms faster than they remove anything.
- Early data shows productivity gains but not structural simplification.

**Revision:**  
In the near term, **AI increases complexity**.  
Simplification comes only after platform consolidation.
